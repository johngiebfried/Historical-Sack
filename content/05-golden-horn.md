# The Golden Horn

*The city's harbour shore: the quays and warehouses, the old Venetian, Pisan and Genoese quarters, the Exchange, and the streets of houses that climb from the water. The Residential and Merchant regions are folded in here and renamed for where nearly all of their events take place. Two scenes stand inland, near the Great Church: Choniates' house (H-Q2) and the Chalkoprateia (H-7). Their source notes say so.*

**Region blurb (student-facing).** The Golden Horn is the city's harbour: quays and warehouses, the old quarters where Venetians, Pisans and Genoese lived until last year, and streets of houses climbing from the water toward the Great Church. The richest families left at dawn. Everyone else is waiting to see what the Latins will do.

**Special rule: the seventh path.** Besides positions 1–6, any player in this region may choose **position 7: St Simeon**. It takes no slot from the thirty, and any number of players can choose it. Everyone who takes it shares in the theft. It exists for the Venetian characters whose secret objective is the saint's body.

## H-Q1 · Old Neighbours

*Shared question 1 · Monday 12 April, evening · Every choice ends the same way: the quarter falls, and old scores are settled around you.*

**Prompt.** With the army come Latins who used to live in this quarter until they were driven out last summer: Venetians, Pisans, Germans, Frenchmen. They know these streets and the people in them, and some have come back for revenge. One of them is dragging a Greek neighbour out of his house by the hair, and others are gathering round.

**A · Violence.** Join them. These Greeks turned on the Latins before.
> The returning men welcome your sword. What follows is a settling of accounts twenty years old, and you take a share of the dead man's house for your trouble.
`marks 100 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**B · Piety.** Put yourself between them and the Greek.
> The returning men curse you for taking the side of the people who robbed them, but they won't fight a crusader in mail. The Greek's family pulls him back inside and bars the door.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**C · Plunder.** Stand back, let them settle it, and ask them which houses on the street are the richest.
> They know every door, and they tell you. You leave them to their business and go to the houses they named.
`marks 150 · fama -1 · nefas 0`

**D · Sacrilege.** Follow them to the parish church, where the neighbours have taken refuge, and help break in the doors.
> The doors give. Whatever sanctuary the church once offered, it offers none tonight.
`marks 100 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: **P** for the killing; attested for the expulsion.*

- **The killing.** Gunther ch. 18, via your paper: almost two thousand citizens fell, not at the crusaders' hands, Gunther insists, but at the hands of Franks, Italians, Venetians, Germans and others who had once lived in the city and had been expelled as suspected traitors at the time of the siege. Your paper places this on 12 April, during the fighting, not during the sack.
- **The expulsion.** Andrea's note to the *Devastatio*, in your sources file: Western merchants resident in the city, especially Genoese, Venetians and Pisans, took refuge with the crusaders after the great fire of August 1203.
- **The grievance.** The Game Book's reading on the massacre of the Latins in 1182 supplies it.
- **Killing that day.** Choniates (Mag. 313): the sword was drawn against every age and sex.
- **Roster.** Domenico of Constantinople (#52) is himself a resident Latin.

## H-Q2 · The House with the Low Porch

*Shared question 2 · 13–17 April · Every choice ends the same way: the household leaves the city in the end.*

**Prompt.** Near the Great Church, behind a dark doorway under a low porch, a crowd of Greek gentlefolk has taken refuge in a borrowed house. A Venetian in armour at the door tells you, in your own language, that he took it first and it is his. Behind him you glimpse their host: Niketas Choniates, one of the highest officials of the empire, whose own great house burned in last summer's fire.

**A · Piety.** Accept his claim and move on.
> The Venetian nods, and you go. Inside, a family of Greeks breathes again, for now.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**B · Sacrilege.** Push past him to the household's icon corner, and strip the silver covers from the icons.
> The faces of the saints are left bare on their boards. The Venetian can't stop you, and the Greeks don't try.
`marks 100 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**C · Violence.** Call his bluff, beat him back, and take the household as your prisoners, with Choniates first.
> The Venetian is one man, and he gives way. You hold Choniates and his household for five days, until his friends pay a ransom. On Saturday he walks out of the city with the others. In the meantime he has told you more about the empire's tax registers than he meant to.
`marks 150 · fama -1 · nefas +2 · bonus: Choniates Held (shared by every player who chooses C; see rule below) · tags: named-person`

**D · Plunder.** Strike a bargain with the Venetian: half of what the Greeks have, and the house is his.
> He agrees, because he can't hold the door against you, and he hates you for it. The Greeks pay. The house stays his.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Rule: Choniates Held (proposed).**

- **Bonus.** Every player who chose C shares the ransom, already in the marks above.
- **Class-wide effect.** The class learns what the empire's tax registers hold. When the partition is decided, the GM may treat the crusaders as already having the registers that the historical Latins had to commission assessors to compile.
- **No contradiction with Blachernae.** The ransom outcome lets Choniates still leave on 17 April and meet the Blachernae players at the gate (B-Q3).

**GM note.** If the student playing Domenico (#52) is in this region, this is their own door. Read the scene to them from the doorway, with the choices as what they do when the French come: A holds the door, B fails to stop the icons being stripped, C gives the household up, D strikes the bargain. Keep the same rewards. Choice A is what Choniates says his Venetian friend did.

**Source note.** *Status: attested; Choniates' capture is counterfactual.*

- **The house.** Choniates (Mag. 322–23): on the day the city fell, friends crowded into his house, whose dark and hard-to-find entrance lay under a low portico. His great house in the Sphorakion district had burned in the second fire. A Venetian acquaintance put on armour, pretended to be one of the soldiers, told them in their own language that he had taken the house first, and beat off the looters. When the French came in larger numbers he could no longer hold them, so he led the family to a house of Venetian acquaintances and then out, as though they were his captives.
- **The rack.** Mag. 326: his household escaped the rack, by which many were forced to give up their money.
- **The tax registers.** Mag. 327: before the partition the Latins commissioned assessors to register the taxable lands.
- **Domenico.** Your paper names the Venetian Domenico, and the roster's Domenico of Constantinople (#52) is presumably him.
- **Location.** The borrowed house stood near the Great Church, inland from the Horn. It stays in this region, where you placed it.
- **Choniates' office.** **M:** I believe he was grand logothete, but that is memory and needs checking.

## H-1 · Position 1 · The Best House on the Street

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · Tier: highest.*

**Prompt.** Word has gone round the host that no one is to take a house until the leaders decide how to share them out. But the lords' men are already marking their masters' signs on the best doors. On this street stands a great house whose owner, one of the richest men in the city, left at dawn.

**A.** Mark your own sign on the door, and hold it.
> You post a man on the door and your sign above it. When the houses are written down, this one is written against your name, and the lords' men have to go elsewhere.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas 0 · prize: A Great House in Constantinople (a claim to be confirmed at the partition)`

**B.** Strip it before the lords' men arrive.
> By the time the marquis's sergeant comes to mark the door, there is nothing inside worth the chalk.
`marks 400 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Tell the poor knights and sergeants what the lords are doing, and lead them in.
> The news runs through the ranks like fire. The poor of the host go every man for himself, and this house is only the first of many. You have started something, and they know it was you.
`marks 200 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**D.** Wait for the allotment, as ordered.
> You obey. By the time the allotment comes, the best houses have long since been taken by men who didn't.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The order and the seizure.** Clari ch. 80: it was cried through the host that no one should take a house until it was decided how they would be divided. The rich men then met secretly and seized the best houses before the poor knights and the commons knew. When the poor found out, they went every man for himself and took what they could, and there were plenty of houses left.
- **Who left.** The richest townspeople departed, and no harm was done to poor or rich after the city was taken.
- **Your paper.** This is the evidence at the centre of your argument that the sack began with this broken promise.
- **Option C.** In effect it lets the student start the rush that Clari describes.
- **Roster.** Robert of Clari (#5) is the voice of the poor knights here.

## H-2 · Position 2 · The Exchange

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** Before the Exchange stand two bronze women, twenty feet high. One points west, and her inscription says that the city's conquerors will come from there. The other points to a filthy place and says that is where they will be thrown out again. Inside, where money-changers used to sit behind heaps of coin and gems, only a few remain, packing up in haste.

**A.** Break open a money-changer's strongbox.
> It takes an axe and a little time. The box is full of gold coin and loose stones.
`marks 450 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**B.** Buy the changers' gems at a fraction of their worth as they flee.
> They know they are being cheated, and they take your coin anyway, because coin can be hidden more easily than gems.
`marks 300 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Escort the changers to safety, for a fee.
> You see three families of money-changers through the streets to the waterfront. They pay well, and they pay willingly.
`marks 150 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**D.** Have both inscriptions read to you, and carry word of them to the bishops.
> The bishops are pleased with the first prophecy and uneasy about the second. They remember who brought it.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The statues.** Clari ch. 91: two copper images of women, each at least twenty feet high. One stretched her hand toward the West, inscribed that from the West would come those who would conquer Constantinople. The other pointed to an unseemly place, saying that there they would be thrown out again.
- **The Exchange.** The statues stood before the Exchange, where rich money-changers used to sit with heaps of bezants and precious stones, but not so many of them now that the city was taken.

## H-3 · Position 3 · The Buried Silver

## H-5 · Position 5 · The Buyer

*Unique question 3 · 13–17 April · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** A Greek dealer is going from billet to billet buying plunder, and he is not particular. He will take church plate as readily as household silver. Once it has left a church, he says, it is only metal.

**A.** Sell him your church silver.
> He weighs it, pays fairly by weight, and wraps the chalices in sacking. Whatever he does with them, they are his sin now as well as yours.
`marks 250 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**B.** Sell him only household goods.
> Plates, cups and a lamp. He pays less than you hoped and more than you expected.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Refuse, and warn your comrades off him.
> He moves on to the next billet, where someone will certainly sell. Your company thinks you are strange, but honest.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas -1`

**D.** Take his purse.
> He came to buy, and he leaves with nothing. Word goes round the dealers to avoid your street.
`marks 200 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **Why it's here.** It is one of the few places where Choniates shows Greeks profiting from the sack, so it gives Greeks agency in the region.

## H-7 · Position 7 · St Simeon (special path)

*Unique question 3 · the days after 13 April · Not one of the thirty slots; open to any number of players in this region · Everyone who takes it shares the prize.*

**Prompt.** You haven't come for silver. You and a few Venetians from the parish of San Simeone in Venice know that the body of the prophet Simeon lies in a church near the Great Church, and you mean to bring him home. The church is quiet, and its clergy are frightened. The leaders' guards are everywhere, looking for anyone who keeps back booty.

**A.** Go by night, and break into the crypt.
> You lift the stone by lamplight and wrap the saint in a sail. Nobody sees you go, but you know what you broke to get him.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas +2 · prize: The Relics of St Simeon (shared by everyone on this path; fulfils the Venetian secret objective) · tags: relic`

**B.** Bribe the sacristan to show you the tomb.
> The sacristan takes your silver and your promise that the saint will be honoured in Venice. He shows you the stair himself.
`marks -100 · fama 0 · nefas +1 · prize: The Relics of St Simeon (shared by everyone on this path; fulfils the Venetian secret objective) · tags: relic`

**C.** Carry him out in daylight, telling the Frankish guards he is a Venetian saint going home.
> It is a bold lie, and partly true. The guards wave you through, and the whole quarter sees the procession.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas +1 · prize: The Relics of St Simeon (shared by everyone on this path; fulfils the Venetian secret objective) · tags: relic`

**D.** Hide him in a crate, and swear to the ship's master that it holds glass.
> The master doesn't ask twice. The saint sails for Venice packed in straw.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas +1 · prize: The Relics of St Simeon (shared by everyone on this path; fulfils the Venetian secret objective) · tags: relic`

**Source note.** *Status: **P**. The *Translatio Symonensis* isn't in your sources file. This rests on the brief's reading of Perry.*

- **The theft.** Parishioners of San Simeone Profeta in Venice stole the relics from the church of St Mary Chalkoprateia, beside Hagia Sophia (Perry, *Sacred Plunder*, p. 98 and p. 119, as cited in the brief).
- **The thieves.** The brief names Steno and Andrea Drusiaco among them. Your paper cites Perry's article as "…and the Seven Thieves." Check the number.
- **How dangerous was it?** Perry reads the text as inflating a danger that probably didn't exist, since only the crusade leaders could have caught the thieves. Option A's quiet success follows that reading.
- **Option D.** It borrows the trick from another Venetian relic narrative, the *Translatio Pauli*, in which the thief tells the captain the box holds glass (brief, citing Perry).
- **Location.** The IM places the tomb in the Greek Residential Quarter (line 883), which this region replaces. The Chalkoprateia actually stood beside the Great Church, inland from the Horn. The seventh path keeps the saint in this region, as you decided, and names the right church.
- **Roster.** Pietro Steno (#22) and Andrea Balduino (#39), both of the parish of San Simeone per the Game Book. Matteo Steno (#44), Pietro's uncle, knows of the plot but takes no part in it.
- **Who Simeon was.** **M:** I take him to be the Simeon of Luke 2, who held the infant Christ, but that is memory and needs checking.
