# Petrion: The Breach

*The quarter behind the Golden Horn sea walls where the army broke in on Monday 12 April. It includes the hill of the Pantepoptes monastery, where the emperor's camp stood; the Pantokrator monastery; and, as the IM draws the region, the Church of the Holy Apostles.*

**Region blurb (student-facing).** You came through the sea wall with Peter of Amiens' company. Behind you are the ships; ahead are the hill where the emperor watched the assault, and beyond it some of the richest churches in Christendom.

---

## P-Q1 · The Postern

*Shared question 1 · Monday 12 April, afternoon · Every choice ends the same way: you are through, and the defenders break.*

**Prompt.** The walled-up postern finally gives, and the hole is barely wide enough for a body. Through it you can see the defenders packed into the lane beyond, with more on the wall above hurling stones down on the breach. You are first through, on hands and knees, and they have seen you.

**A · Violence.** Draw your knife and charge them, roaring, before they can close on you.
> They had expected the hole to stay empty. One crusader coming at them with a blade and a bellow is enough. The front rank breaks, the rest follow, and they run like driven cattle while your company pours through behind you, shouting your name.
`marks 0 · fama +3 · nefas 0`

**B · Piety.** Turn back to the breach and tear at the loose stones to widen it, calling on God and the Virgin to bring your comrades through.
> Stones crack on the wall beside your head while you work, but the hole opens, and a dozen men come through at once. The defenders see the numbers and give way. Your company credits the breach to you and to heaven, roughly in that order.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**C · Plunder.** Go for what the front rank has dropped in the mud: gilded shields, axes, a paymaster's satchel.
> The fighting moves up the lane without you. You come away with a gilded shield and a satchel of Greek coin. The men who did the fighting noticed where you were.
`marks 100 · fama -1 · nefas 0`

**D · Sacrilege.** Above the inner face of the gate hangs an icon of the Virgin, set there to guard it. Wrench it down and brandish it at the defenders.
> The defenders see their gate's protector in a Latin fist and lose heart. Your comrades cheer the trick. The priests in the host are less pleased with how you handled her, and you pry the silver frame loose before anyone asks for it.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: the breach is attested; the four ways through are adapted.*

- **The breach.** Clari chs. 75–76: Peter of Amiens, with about ten knights and sixty sergeants, hacked through a walled-up false postern under bolts, stones, boiling pitch and Greek fire. Aleaumes of Clari, a cleric, crawled through while his brother Robert held his foot to stop him. He charged the crowd with his knife and they fled.
- **The Greek view.** Choniates (Mag. 313) remembers one giant knight, Peter, with a helmet like a towered city, routing thousands from the hill.
- **Option D.** The gate icon is invented. The idea that a captured icon could break Byzantine morale comes from Clari ch. 66, where the crusaders rowed the captured imperial icon along the walls in a galley.
- **Bias.** Clari is telling his brother's heroics. Choniates is shaming Byzantine cowardice. Both magnify the moment.
- **Roster.** Peter of Amiens (#29) and Robert of Clari (#5) were at this gate.

## P-Q2 · The Emperor's Camp

*Shared question 2 · Monday 12 April, late afternoon · Every choice ends the same way: the emperor flees, and his camp on the hill is taken.*

**Prompt.** Inside the wall, Peter's handful of knights and sergeants form up on foot. On the hill by the Pantepoptes monastery the emperor's scarlet tents stand open. Mourtzouphlos himself spurs toward you with silver trumpets blaring, then reins in halfway, as if waiting to see whether you will break.

**A · Piety.** Hold your place in Peter's line, and let the emperor come or go as God wills.
> The line holds. The emperor turns back to his tents, then abandons them, and behind you someone breaks open a gate so the knights can lead their horses in. What you pick up in the camp you hand to your lord for the common stock, as you swore.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**B · Sacrilege.** Make for the chapel tent, where the emperor's campaign icons and a gilded processional cross stand unguarded.
> You strip the silver and stones from the icon frames and leave the painted boards in the grass. It is a good haul. Word of where it came from travels through the host faster than you do.
`marks 150 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**C · Violence.** Charge after the emperor's retreating guard and cut down the stragglers on the slope.
> The guard does not stand. You catch the slowest of them among the tents and come back with a captain's sword belt. The emperor is already gone into the city.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**D · Plunder.** Go straight for the tents: the coffers, the plate, the jewels.
> You are among the first into the pavilion, with its chests of plate and jewelry. You carry off what you can before your lords' men arrive to claim the rest.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested; the chapel tent is invented.*

- **The camp and the half-charge.** Clari ch. 70: scarlet tents on a hillock, silver trumpets and timbrels. Ch. 77: the emperor spurs halfway toward Peter's men, and Peter tells them not to give ground. Ch. 78: the emperor turns back, a gate is broken open, the horses are brought in, and he flees, leaving tents, coffers and jewels, which Peter of Bracieux takes.
- **Villehardouin** ("The crusaders take a part of the city"; §§244–45): the emperor flees through the streets to the Bucoleon, and Count Baldwin sleeps that night in the scarlet tents.
- **Choniates** (Mag. 313): the imperial pavilion stood on the hill of the Pantepoptes. The Latins despoiled it and made the monastery their headquarters.
- **The chapel tent.** Invented, but Byzantine emperors did take icons into battle (Clari ch. 66).
- **Conflict.** Clari gives the camp's spoils to Peter of Bracieux; Villehardouin puts Baldwin in the tents.
- **Roster.** Peter of Bracieux (#40) and Baldwin of Flanders (#3).

## P-1 · Position 1 · Constantine's Tomb

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · Holy Apostles · Tier: highest.*

**Prompt.** The Church of the Holy Apostles is said to be even richer than Hagia Sophia. Here lie the bodies of apostles, the pillar at which Christ was scourged, and the tombs of the emperors. Constantine, who founded this city, lies here with his mother Helena. A single old monk keeps watch beside the great porphyry sarcophagus. Nobody else has found it yet.

**A.** Break the seal and take the crown from Constantine's head.
> Under nine centuries of dust lies a crown. Whoever holds the crown of the first Christian emperor holds an argument no elector can ignore. The monk's cry carries through the church, though, and the Greeks will speak of this desecration for generations.
`marks 0 · fama +4 · nefas +4 · prize: The Crown of Constantine (+1 vote in the imperial election) · tags: regalia`

**B.** Take the relics of the apostles from their shrines.
> You fill a chest with the bones of the apostles, wrapped in silk. Any church in the West would build a new chapel to receive them, and their abbots will remember who sent them.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas +3 · prize: Relics of the Apostles (+1 vote on any committee) · tags: relic`

**C.** Strip the gold and gems from the other imperial tombs, but leave Constantine's sealed.
> The lesser emperors yield gold, pearls and stones. You leave the founder alone, which seems to satisfy your conscience more than it satisfies the monk.
`marks 400 · fama 0 · nefas +2`

**D.** Kneel beside the monk and keep watch over the tombs through the night.
> No one disturbs the tombs while you watch. In the morning the monk gives you his blessing and a few coins from the alms box, and you leave with a story worth more than the coins.
`marks 50 · fama +2 · nefas -2`

**Source note.** *Status: setting attested; the crown is invented.*

- **The church.** Clari ch. 87: richer than Hagia Sophia; the bodies of apostles; the pillar of the Scourging; Constantine, Helena and other emperors buried there.
- **When the tombs were robbed.** Choniates (Mag. 357, the *De Signis*): the Latins broke open the imperial tombs by night and found Justinian's body uncorrupted, yet still robbed it. He places this after the Venetian patriarch arrived, so it is not part of the sack itself. Your paper adds that Alexios III plundered the same tombs to pay off Henry VI, stopping short of Constantine's; it dates this to 1196, while the brief, citing Angold, gives 1197.
- **The prize.** Nothing describes the contents of Constantine's sarcophagus, so the crown is invented. It replaces the IM's ring and sword and keeps its +1 imperial vote. The IM puts the Holy Apostles in the Petrion region, although the church stood on the fourth hill.

## P-2 · Position 2 · Manuel's Tomb

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · An abbey of the Petrion (the Pantokrator?) · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** In one of the great abbeys lies the emperor Manuel, in a tomb richer than any saint's. Beside it stands a slab of red marble, the stone on which Christ's body was laid when it was taken down from the Cross. The monks show you what they say are the Virgin's tears, still marked on its surface.

**A.** Strip Manuel's tomb of its gold and stones.
> The tomb is everything they said. You take what can be pried loose and leave the old emperor lying in a stripped case.
`marks 400 · fama 0 · nefas +3`

**B.** Claim the stone of the Deposition for your church at home.
> It will take a cart, a ship and a great deal of explaining, but the stone on which Christ lay is yours to send west. No relic in your country could match it.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas +2 · prize: The Stone of the Deposition (+1 vote on any committee; must be shipped, so cannot be sold in the city) · tags: relic`

**C.** Pry the gems from the stone's setting and leave the slab.
> The setting was worth more than you expected. The monks watch you do it and say nothing at all, which is worse than shouting.
`marks 250 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

**D.** Stand guard at the tomb so that no one strips it, and accept the monks' gratitude.
> Three parties of looters come and go while you stand there. The monks give you a meal and a small purse, and they pray for you by name.
`marks 50 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**Source note.** *Status: setting attested; the abbey's identity needs checking.*

- **The source.** Clari ch. 92: in an abbey of the city lay the emperor Manuel, more richly entombed than any saint, and there too was the marble table on which Christ was laid after the Deposition, with the Virgin's tears visible on it. Clari doesn't name the abbey.
- **The abbey.** **M:** I identify it as the Pantokrator, the Komnenian burial monastery, but that is memory. **M:** I can't say from the sources in hand what became of the stone; that needs checking.

## P-3 · Position 3 · The Old Priest

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Pantokrator · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** The Greeks of the district stored their valuables in the Pantokrator for safety, and your comrades are ransacking the main church for gold. In a side chapel you find an old priest with a long white beard, sitting on an iron-bound chest. He says it holds only holy things and begs you to leave it.

**A.** Threaten him with death unless he opens it.
> His hands shake on the lock. Inside lie relics wrapped in silk, each with a scrap of parchment naming its saint. You fill your arms and leave him weeping on the empty chest.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas +2 · prize: A chest of relics (+1 vote on any committee) · tags: relic`

**B.** Speak to him as one Christian to another, and ask him to share what he guards rather than lose it to the men in the church.
> He looks at you for a long time, then decides that holy things are safer in your hands than in the hands of the men shouting next door. He opens the chest and chooses for you himself. Before you go, he asks your name.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0 · prize: A chest of relics (+1 vote on any committee) · tags: relic`

**C.** Push him aside and take the gold and silver reliquaries. Leave the bones.
> The reliquaries are heavy and fine. The bones go back into the chest, loose, and the priest gathers them up as you leave.
`marks 300 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

**D.** Leave him and the chest, and join the others stripping the main church.
> The main church gives up its candlesticks and plate. Behind you the priest has gone back to sitting on his chest.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: **P**, from Gunther, *Hystoria Constantinopolitana* ch. 19, which is not in the sources file. This reading follows your paper and the siege handoff.*

- **The story.** Abbot Martin of Pairis entered the church where valuables of the region had been stored. He threatened an old Greek priest, who gave him relics. Gunther calls it a sacred sacrilege. The priest reasoned that a man of religion was better than bloodstained hands, and he later became Martin's friend.
- **The church.** Your paper identifies it as the Pantokrator, one of the churches where booty was collected.
- **Bias.** Gunther writes a relic-theft narrative to authenticate Pairis's relics and to excuse how they were taken.
- **Roster.** Martin of Pairis (#12). If Martin's player takes this position, option B is Gunther's own account.

## P-4 · Position 4 · The Convent Gate

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · A convent of the Petrion · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** The gate of a convent is barred. Through the grille an old abbess offers you the convent's small treasury if you will keep the men behind you from breaking in. The men behind you say a barred gate means gold, and they are waiting to see what you do.

**A.** Accept the treasury, and stand guard at the gate.
> The abbess passes a bag of silver through the grille. You turn and face your own comrades, and after some argument they go elsewhere.
`marks 150 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**B.** Refuse the gift, and stand guard anyway.
> You tell the abbess to keep her silver for her nuns. The men grumble and leave. That evening the story goes round the camp, and it does you no harm.
`marks 0 · fama +3 · nefas -2`

**C.** Break in with the men.
> What happens inside is what the oath forbade and what Choniates would later lament. You leave with the convent's silver and a share in its shame.
`marks 250 · fama -3 · nefas +3`

**D.** Take the treasury through the grille, then walk away and let the men do as they please.
> You have the silver. You don't look back at the gate.
`marks 150 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**Source note.** *Status: the convent is invented; the conditions are attested.*

- **The oath.** Clari ch. 68: before the assault every man swore on relics to do no violence to any woman, on pain of death, and not to lay hands on monk, cleric or priest, nor break into church or monastery.
- **The charge.** Choniates (Mag. 315) says nuns and maidens were not spared. Your paper notes that both Choniates and Mesarites charge widespread rape, but give only one specific case.
- **Care.** Option C's outcome is told in a single sentence and never depicted. The convent itself stands in for the IM's "Convent of the Petrians."

## P-5 · Position 5 · Guard of the Treasure

*Unique question 3 · the days after 13 April · The collection church · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** The leaders have ordered all booty brought to a guarded abbey, and you are posted at its door with nine other Franks and ten Venetians. On the first night you watch two of your fellow guards slip gold jewelry into their sleeves. One of them catches your eye and holds out a ring.

**A.** Take the ring and say nothing.
> It is a good ring. By morning there are several fewer pieces of gold in the pile, and nobody counts.
`marks 200 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**B.** Report them to the Count of Saint-Pol.
> The count does not wait for a trial. One of the two is hanged with his shield around his neck as a lesson to the host. Some thank you, and some will never trust you again.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**C.** If the guards are helping themselves, help yourself properly.
> You fill a sack and get it to your lodging before dawn. Others are doing the same, which makes it safer and no less a theft.
`marks 400 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**D.** Refuse the ring and keep your watch.
> The guard shrugs and pockets it. You stand your watch to the end, and the pile you guarded is a little smaller in the morning anyway.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested; the scene is adapted.*

- **Where the booty went.** The sources disagree. Clari ch. 81 has one abbey guarded by ten Franks and ten Venetians, and says those very guards took gold jewels. Villehardouin §252 has three churches guarded by Franks and Venetians. The *Devastatio* has three towers filled with silver.
- **The church.** Your paper, citing Gunther (**P**), identifies one of the churches as the Pantokrator.
- **The hanging.** Villehardouin §255: thieves were hanged, and the Count of Saint-Pol hanged one of his own knights with his shield around his neck.
- **Roster.** Hugh of Saint-Pol (#9).

## P-6 · Position 6 · The Cistern

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · Under the Petrion · Tier: lowest · Repeatable.*

**Prompt.** A soldier swears he saw a Greek family carrying bundles down a stair into one of the district's great underground cisterns on the night of the assault. The stair disappears into black water among rows of columns.

**A.** Wade in with a torch.
> Waist-deep and freezing, you find a bundle wedged at the base of a column: silver cups wrapped in a cloak.
`marks 120 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**B.** Wait at the head of the stair for whoever comes back for it.
> At dusk a man and his grown son come down with a lamp. You let them fetch the bundle, then take it from them at the top.
`marks 120 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**C.** Search the cistern-keeper's lodge instead.
> The keeper has fled with whatever he had. You find a few coins and a good knife.
`marks 40 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**D.** Mark the place for the commissioners collecting the booty.
> You tell the clerks where to look. They thank you without much interest.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: the conditions are attested; the hiding place is invented.*

- **The hiding.** Choniates (Mag. 313): on the night of 12 April every citizen was busy removing and burying his possessions. Mag. 322: once the city fell, owners were interrogated about where their treasure was hidden.
- **The cistern.** It replaces the IM's underground lake.
- **Why it can repeat.** It works for two players because there is no exclusive item.
