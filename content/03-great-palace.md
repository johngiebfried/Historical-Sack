# The Great Palace: Bucoleon and Hippodrome

*The palace of the Bucoleon, which Clari calls the Palace of the Lion's Mouth, the Hippodrome beside it, and the approach from the Milion. Boniface took the palace's surrender on 13 April. This region holds the game's highest prizes.*

**Region blurb (student-facing).** The Great Palace is the old heart of the empire: five hundred rooms, thirty chapels, and the most famous relics in the world. To reach it you pass the emperor's guard and the Hippodrome.

---

## G-Q1 · The Axe-Bearers

*Shared question 1 · before dawn, Tuesday 13 April · Every choice ends the same way: the Varangians do not fight, and the road to the palace opens.*

**Prompt.** Before dawn, at the Milion beside the Great Church, a company of the emperor's axe-bearers stands around a young nobleman named Laskaris. Many of them are Englishmen and Danes. Laskaris was chosen emperor by lot a few hours ago, and he is begging them to fight. They are asking what they will be paid. Your company comes up the street in full mail.

**A · Violence.** Charge them before they finish bargaining.
> They are in no mood to die for a promise. They break into the side streets, and you catch the slowest. The way to the palace is open, and your name goes into the story of the morning.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**B · Piety.** Call out to them in their own tongue: fellow Christians, the city is lost; lay down your axes and no one will harm you.
> Some laugh, but most lower their axes and walk away down the hill. A few come over to your banner. Nobody on either side bleeds.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**C · Plunder.** Outbid Laskaris. Offer them silver to stand aside, and take their pay chest as surety.
> They accept your promise and go. You keep the chest, and you were never going to pay the promise.
`marks 200 · fama -1 · nefas 0`

**D · Sacrilege.** Seize the processional cross and icons carried by the clergy beside Laskaris, and hold them up before the guard.
> The clergy scatter, the patriarch escapes in the confusion, and the axe-bearers take the loss of their holy standards as the end of the matter. The cross is heavy with silver.
`marks 100 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: attested; the processional cross is adapted.*

- **The night.** Choniates (Mag. 314): after Mourtzouphlos fled, Doukas and Laskaris contested the throne in the Great Church. Laskaris, chosen by lot, refused the imperial insignia. Escorted by the patriarch to the Milion, he urged the people and the axe-bearing guard to resist, reminding the guard of their pay. They agreed to fight for wages, then fled when the Latin columns appeared in full armour early on 13 April.
- **Laskaris's flight.** Clari ch. 79: Laskaris was made emperor that night and crossed to Nicaea before daybreak.
- **The guard.** Clari ch. 74 has English, Danes and Greeks defending the towers.
- **Option D.** The patriarch was present, but the seizure of the cross is invented. The patriarch himself must escape here, because he rides out on the 17th (Blachernae, B-Q3).

## G-Q2 · Through the Hippodrome

*Shared question 2 · Tuesday 13 April, morning · Every choice ends the same way: you pass through the Hippodrome to the palace gates.*

**Prompt.** The way to the palace runs through the Hippodrome, a racecourse a crossbow-shot and a half long, ringed with thirty or forty tiers of seats. Along the wall down its middle stand bronze men and women, horses, oxen, camels, bears and lions, so lifelike that the Greeks say they once moved. At one end sits a bronze Herakles so huge that a man's belt would barely go round his thumb. A few Greeks are hiding in the upper tiers.

**A · Piety.** Marvel at it, and march on. Whatever is here belongs to the common stock.
> You stop long enough to look, as every Frank does who passes through, and then go on. Clari, gazing at the same bronzes, thought no master in Christendom could match them.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**B · Sacrilege.** In the imperial box above the track is a small oratory of icons. Pull them out to make seats and trophies for a mock race of your own.
> Your comrades race round the turning post with icons for prizes and a footstool of painted saints. The Greeks in the tiers watch it all.
`marks 50 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**C · Violence.** Drive the Greeks out of the tiers, and beat them for what they carry.
> They had little, and they give it up quickly. You leave the tiers emptier and yourself no richer than you hoped.
`marks 100 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**D · Plunder.** Hack off whatever bronze and gilding you can carry from the statues.
> The statues are too big to take, so you settle for a gilded fitting and a bronze paw. The rest will wait for someone with furnaces.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested; the oratory is adapted.*

- **The Hippodrome.** Clari ch. 90: the Games of the Emperor stood beside the Palace of the Lion's Mouth. It was a crossbow-shot and a half long, with thirty or forty tiers of seats and lodges for the emperor and empress. Along the central wall stood copper images of men, women, horses, oxen, camels, bears and lions that once moved by enchantment, and the Franks gazed at them in wonder.
- **The statues' fate.** Choniates (Mag. 358–61): the Latins later melted the Hippodrome bronzes for coin, among them the Herakles whose thumb needed a belt to go round it, the she-wolf, the eagle, Helen, and the bull and basilisk. Your paper dates the melting to Henry, late in 1205, and points out that Clari saw them standing after the sack. **So in April 1204 they were still there,** and no choice here destroys them.
- **Option B.** The oratory is invented. Icons used as footstools and mockery of Greek ceremony come from Choniates (Mag. 326–27).

## G-1 · Position 1 · The Empress

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Bucoleon · Tier: highest.*

**Prompt.** The Bucoleon has surrendered to Boniface on the promise that everyone inside will live. In an inner chamber the noblewomen who fled here are waiting. Among them is the empress Margaret, sister of the King of Hungary and widow of the emperor Isaac, with her small son. Another is the dowager Agnes, sister of the King of France. Margaret asks whom you serve.

**A.** Take Margaret into your own custody as a hostage.
> She comes with you without a word, holding her son's hand. Whoever marries the widowed empress gains a claim on Greek loyalty that no army can buy.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas +2 · prize: Empress Margaret (+2 votes in the imperial election for whoever holds or marries her; counts as "Greek empress" for Boniface in the endgame) · tags: named-person`

**B.** Offer Margaret your protection, and carry her terms to Boniface yourself.
> You stand at her door until Boniface's marshal arrives, and you hand him her terms in person. She thanks you as an empress thanks a knight, and the marquis hears about it.
`marks 0 · fama +3 · nefas -2`

**C.** Demand the ladies' jewels as the price of keeping the soldiers out.
> They strip their rings and collars into your hands. The soldiers stay out, this time.
`marks 400 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**D.** Bow, leave the ladies to Boniface's guard, and search the empty apartments instead.
> The apartments were left in a hurry. You find enough in them.
`marks 200 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**GM note.** Agnes is a playable character (#10) and is never a prize. If the student playing Agnes holds this position, the scene is her own surrender. Read the prompt from inside the room, with the choices recast as whom she will trust: A means accepting a protector's custody, B means negotiating through Boniface, C means buying safety, and D means waiting it out. Keep the same rewards. The prize then becomes Agnes's alliance with Margaret, at the GM's discretion.

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The surrender.** Villehardouin §249: the Bucoleon surrendered to Boniface on condition that the lives of all inside be spared. In it were found many great ladies, among them the sister of the King of France and the sister of the King of Hungary, both of whom had been empresses.
- **Margaret's marriage.** Clari ch. 99: the marquis married the wife of Isaac. Choniates (Mag. 328–30), who calls her Maria, says she became Boniface's wife and that Boniface proclaimed her first-born son Manuel emperor to win over the Greeks.
- **The endgame.** The IM's endgame line for Boniface with a Greek empress needs this prize. It replaces Blachernae 1, which Choniates contradicts.

## G-2 · Position 2 · The Regalia

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Bucoleon · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** In a robing room near the throne hall, the coronation vestments lie in their chests: a long mantle heavy with stones, a cloak worked with eagles made of jewels, scarlet hose and jewelled shoes, the crown, and a brooch that the emperor Manuel is said to have bought for sixty-two thousand marks.

**A.** Take the whole set for yourself.
> No one can be crowned properly without these, and now no one can be crowned without you. The leaders will not forgive it quickly, and they will not be able to ignore it either.
`marks 0 · fama -1 · nefas +1 · prize: The Imperial Regalia (a bargaining piece: the GM rules what the next emperor will give for it) · tags: regalia`

**B.** Keep the set together, and guarded, for whoever is crowned.
> When the time comes, the bishops find every piece where it should be. The new emperor knows who kept them.
`marks 0 · fama +3 · nefas -1`

**C.** Pry the stones out of the mantle and the cloak.
> A fortune in stones, and two ruined vestments.
`marks 500 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**D.** Take only Manuel's brooch.
> It is small enough to hide in your fist and worth more than a county, if half the stories are true.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas +1 · prize: Manuel's Brooch (worth 500 marks if sold; worth a great deal more as a gift to the right candidate, at the GM's discretion) · tags: regalia`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The vestments.** Clari ch. 96: at Baldwin's coronation he wore scarlet samite hose, shoes set with stones, the imperial mantle laden with precious stones, and a cloak whose eagles were made of jewels. He was crowned, and a stone that Manuel had bought for sixty-two thousand marks was hung at his neck. Ch. 97 adds the sceptre and the golden apple.
- **They survived.** Baldwin was crowned on 16 May (Villehardouin; Mag. 328), so in fact the set came through the sack intact. Option A is counterfactual.
- **The older crowns** are at Blachernae (B-1).

## G-3 · Position 3 · The Holy Chapel

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Bucoleon · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** Through a door whose hinges are solid silver you enter the palace's Holy Chapel. Its pillars are jasper and porphyry, and its floor is white marble as clear as crystal. In its niches lie the greatest relics in Christendom: two pieces of the True Cross as thick as a man's leg, the lance, the nails, a phial of Christ's blood, his tunic, the crown of thorns, and the head of John the Baptist. An old Greek chaplain stands between you and them.

**A.** Take the True Cross and the Crown of Thorns for yourself.
> You carry the holiest objects in the world out into the courtyard. Within an hour the whole host knows, and the bishops, the barons and your own comrades are shouting at you. They cannot agree what to do with you, and none of them will forget it.
`marks 0 · fama -3 · nefas +4 · prize: The True Cross and the Crown of Thorns (+2 votes on any committee; the endgame relic rules apply if they leave the city) · tags: relic`

**B.** Take a single thorn from the crown, and leave the rest.
> One thorn, sharp as a blade, wrapped in silk. The chaplain sees you take it and says nothing. A bishop at home would give a great deal for it.
`marks 0 · fama 0 · nefas +1 · prize: A Thorn of the Crown (+1 vote on any committee) · tags: relic`

**C.** Take the gold and silver reliquaries, and leave the relics on the altar.
> The reliquaries are worth a fortune by weight. The relics lie on the altar, bare, and the chaplain begins to gather them up.
`marks 500 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

**D.** Stay with the chaplain, and guard the chapel until Boniface's men arrive.
> Two parties of soldiers come to the door and turn back when they see you. When the marquis's men arrive, the chapel is as it was.
`marks 0 · fama +3 · nefas -2`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The chapel.** Clari ch. 82: the Holy Chapel had silver hinges and fittings, pillars of jasper and porphyry, and a floor of white marble like crystal. In it were found two pieces of the True Cross as thick as a man's leg and a fathom long, the lance, the two nails, Christ's blood in a crystal phial, his tunic, the crown of thorns, which Clari says was made of sea rushes as sharp as daggers, the Virgin's robe and the Baptist's head. Ch. 83 adds the towel and the tile with Christ's image, and an icon of St Demetrius that ran with oil.
- **What happened to the relics.** Your paper shows that they stayed in imperial custody and were given away or pledged by Latin emperors later. The Crown of Thorns was pledged and then redeemed by Louis IX. **Option A is counterfactual,** and the endgame's relic rule punishes it.
- **Option B.** Nivelon of Soissons sent a single thorn from the crown to Soissons (Anonymous of Soissons, in your sources file), so a single thorn really did go west.
- **An earlier defence.** Your paper notes that in 1200 the chapel's sacristan, Nicholas Mesarites, defended these relics against Byzantine looters during John Komnenos' coup.

## G-4 · Position 4 · The Treasury

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Bucoleon · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** The treasure in this palace is past counting, and Boniface has set his own men over it. The guard on the vault door is from your own country, and he is bored.

**A.** Bribe him, and take a chest.
> He looks the other way for the price of a good horse. The chest is heavier than the horse.
`marks 400 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**B.** Talk your way in as a messenger from the marquis.
> You walk in with a straight face and out with a small coffer. It is not stealing if no one asks, you tell yourself.
`marks 300 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Report the guard's slackness to Boniface's marshal.
> The guard is replaced, and the marshal learns your name. The treasure is a little safer for a while.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**D.** Join the guard, and keep your hands clean.
> You stand watch through the night beside a fortune and go to bed with nothing.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: the conditions are attested; the guard is invented.*

- **The treasure.** Villehardouin §249: there was so much treasure in the Bucoleon that it could not be counted. §250: each lord set a guard over the treasure of his palace.
- **Guards who stole.** Clari ch. 81: the guards appointed over the booty stole from it.

## G-5 · Position 5 · The Empress's Boat

*Unique question 3 · first light, Tuesday 13 April · The palace quay · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** At the palace's own landing on the sea, servants are loading the last chests into a boat. They tell you that the empress Euphrosyne and her daughters sailed in the night with the emperor Mourtzouphlos, in a small fishing boat. One veiled young woman among the servants keeps her face turned away from you.

**A.** Pull back her veil. She might be one of the daughters.
> She is a maid of the household, and terrified. The daughters really are gone. You have frightened a servant, and the others saw it.
`marks 50 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**B.** Seize the chests before the boat can leave.
> The chests hold the empress's linens, plate and a casket of rings she could not take in the night.
`marks 300 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Let the servants go, with what they have.
> They push off and row hard for the Asian shore. You stand on the quay and watch them go.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas -1`

**D.** Press the servants for where the emperor went.
> They say he means to join his father-in-law, the old emperor Alexios, in Thrace. You carry the news to the barons, who are glad to have it.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: the flight is attested; the quay scene is adapted.*

- **The flight.** Choniates (Mag. 314): Mourtzouphlos went into the Great Palace, put the empress Euphrosyne, wife of Alexios III, and her daughters on board a small fishing boat, and sailed away on the night of 12–13 April. **So the daughters were gone before any Latin reached the palace,** and every choice here agrees with that.
- **Conflict.** Villehardouin (§246) says he left by the Golden Gate.
- **What happened next.** Villehardouin's chapter titles record that he took refuge with Alexios III, who had him blinded. The *Deeds of the Bishops of Halberstadt* adds that the Latins later captured him and threw him down to his death in the city.
- **The quay.** **M:** I'm relying on memory that the Bucoleon had its own harbour. Its servants and last boat are invented.

## G-6 · Position 6 · Five Hundred Rooms

*Unique question 3 · Tuesday 13 April · The Bucoleon · Tier: lowest · Repeatable.*

**Prompt.** The palace runs on and on: five hundred rooms opening one into the next, every wall in gold mosaic, and thirty chapels large and small. Others have been through already, but the place is too big to empty in a day.

**A.** Pull down the silk hangings.
> Bolts of silk, lightly used. They will sell well in the camp.
`marks 100 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**B.** Search the smaller chapels for anything silver.
> Lamps, a censer, a small cross. The chapels were not all found first.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**C.** Find a quiet room, and claim it as your lodging.
> A room with a gold ceiling and a view of the sea, at least until someone more important arrives.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**D.** Pry gold tesserae out of the walls.
> It is gilded glass, nearly worthless once it is out of the wall. You have spoiled a wall that took an emperor's lifetime to make.
`marks 25 · fama -1 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The palace.** Clari ch. 82: the Palace of the Lion's Mouth held five hundred chambers, all joined to one another and wrought in gold mosaic, and some thirty chapels.
- **Option D.** The material of the tesserae, gilded glass, is general knowledge rather than from a source.
