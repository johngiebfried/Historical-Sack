# Blachernae: Henry's Palace and the Land Walls

*The palace complex at the north end of the land walls, where Henry of Flanders took the surrender on 13 April, together with the church of St Mary of Blachernae and the gates where refugees left the city.*

**This region changes the format, as you asked.** Q1 is shared (the surrender). **Q2 is the unique scene** (six positions). **Q3 is shared again**, because the refugees leave after everything else.

**Region blurb (student-facing).** Blachernae was the emperors' favourite palace, set against the great land walls. It is surrendering this morning. In a few days the gates beside it will fill with Greeks leaving the city.

---

## B-Q1 · The Palace Yields

*Shared question 1 · Tuesday 13 April, morning · Every choice ends the same way: Blachernae surrenders to Henry of Flanders, and his men hold it.*

**Prompt.** At the gates of the Blachernae palace, Greek officials come out to Henry of Flanders and offer him the palace on a single condition: no one inside is to be harmed. Henry agrees and begins posting his own men on the gates and the treasury. You are near the front, and the doors are open.

**A · Violence.** Push past the terms. Pull courtiers out of the line and search them for what they carry.
> You come away with rings and purses before Henry's sergeants pull you back. Henry has given his word, and he doesn't thank you for testing it in front of the Greeks.
`marks 100 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**B · Piety.** Stand on Henry's word, and see the people inside come out unharmed.
> You walk the line as the household files out, and you turn back two of your own comrades. Henry notices who kept his promise for him.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**C · Plunder.** Slip in by a side door before the guards are posted.
> The palace is huge and the guards are few. By the time Henry's men reach the upper rooms, you have already been through two of them.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**D · Sacrilege.** While everyone watches the main gate, break into the treasury of the palace chapel.
> Chalices, a jewelled Gospel cover and a censer go into your sack. The chapel's priest saw your face.
`marks 200 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The surrender.** Villehardouin §250: as the Bucoleon surrendered to Boniface, Blachernae surrendered to Henry, Count Baldwin's brother, on condition that no harm be done to those inside. Much treasure was found there. Each lord garrisoned the palace given to him and set a guard on its treasure.
- **Conflict.** Choniates (Mag. 313) says the Latins took Blachernae by assault without difficulty on the 12th.
- **The treasure.** Clari ch. 83 describes it: crowns, jewels, imperial robes.
- **Roster.** Henry of Flanders (#20).

## B-1 · Position 1 · The Crowns of the Old Emperors

*Unique question 2 · Tuesday 13 April · Tier: highest.*

**Prompt.** Deep in the palace, a strongroom holds what the emperors who lived here before left behind: crowns of gold and stones on cushions, jewelled robes of silk, collars and rings. Henry's men have not reached this corridor yet.

**A.** Take the finest crown for yourself.
> A crown worn by emperors is more than gold. Whoever is chosen to rule here will want it, and will pay for it in lands or favour.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas +1 · prize: An Emperor's Crown (a bargaining piece: worth one favour, a fief or 500 marks from the new emperor, at the GM's discretion) · tags: regalia`

**B.** Pry the stones out of every crown and leave the gold.
> You come away with your purse heavy with stones. The crowns stay on their cushions, broken.
`marks 450 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**C.** Take the silk robes, which will sell anywhere in the world.
> They pack small and they are worth a fortune. You look like a pedlar on the way out, which is a reasonable disguise.
`marks 300 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**D.** Fetch Henry, and stand guard on the strongroom until he arrives.
> Henry looks at the room for a long time, then at you. He sees you rewarded from it, and he remembers your name.
`marks 100 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **The strongroom.** Clari ch. 83: in the palace of Blachernae were found the rich crowns of the emperors who had lived there before, their gold jewelry, rich silk robes, imperial robes and precious stones.
- **The regalia that mattered.** The set used for the coronation (Great Palace, G-2) is separate. Baldwin was crowned in it on 16 May.

## B-2 · Position 2 · Friday at St Mary of Blachernae

*Unique question 2 · Friday 16 April · Tier: high (in fama, not marks).*

**Prompt.** It is Friday. In the church of St Mary of Blachernae, the Greeks say, the shroud in which Christ was wrapped raises itself upright every Friday, so that his form can be seen. The clergy are gone. The niche where it hung is open.

**A.** Wait out the day in the church to see it rise.
> The light moves across the floor and nothing happens. The niche is empty. Whatever was there is gone, and nobody can tell you where.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**B.** Search the church and the sacristy for where the clergy hid it.
> You find vestments, lamps and a pair of silver candlesticks, but no shroud.
`marks 100 · fama 0 · nefas +1`

**C.** Take the gold and silver from the empty reliquary niche.
> The fittings are rich even with the relic gone. You leave the niche bare.
`marks 250 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

**D.** Find the clergy who stayed and demand to know where it went.
> A frightened deacon swears he doesn't know, and you come to believe him. Nobody knows.
`marks 0 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**Item card: The Lost Shroud.** Every player at this position receives the card, whatever they choose. It reads: *You looked for it. No one, Greek or Frank, knows what became of it when the city was taken.*

**Source note.** *Status: attested; the scene is dated by calculation.*

- **The shroud.** Clari ch. 92: in the church of St Mary of Blachernae was the shroud in which Christ was wrapped, which every Friday raised itself upright so that his form could be seen, and no one, Greek or Frank, knew what became of it when the city was taken.
- **The date.** Villehardouin dates the fall to Monday 12 April, so the first Friday was 16 April.
- **What is kept out.** Modern theories linking this shroud to later relics are not part of the scene.

## B-3 · Position 3 · The Gate Tower

*Unique question 2 · Tuesday 13 April · On the land walls by the palace · Tier: high.*

**Prompt.** Where the palace meets the land walls, a gate tower stands empty, its garrison fled. A tower like this could become a lordship in the new empire, if someone holds it before the land is divided. Below, Greeks are already asking whether this gate will let them out.

**A.** Plant your banner and hold the tower with your men.
> You post your men on the stairs and your banner on the roof. When the commissioners come round, the tower is written down against your name.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0 · prize: A Fortress Claim (a tower on the land walls, to be confirmed at the partition)`

**B.** Hold the tower, and charge everyone leaving by its gate a toll.
> Families pay a mark or two, and the richer ones pay more. By nightfall you have a chest of silver and a reputation among the Greeks of Thrace.
`marks 300 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

**C.** Hold the tower, and let the refugees pass freely.
> The gate stays open, and the line moves through without your men touching it. The tower is still yours.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1 · prize: A Fortress Claim (a tower on the land walls, to be confirmed at the partition)`

**D.** Strip the armoury and move on.
> Crossbows, good mail and a chest of bolts. Someone else can be lord of an empty tower.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: the conditions are attested; the tower is invented.*

- **Garrisons.** Villehardouin §250: each lord garrisoned the palace surrendered to him.
- **Leaving the city.** Choniates (Mag. 322): once the houses were taken, the chiefs allowed those who wished to leave. Mag. 323–24: soldiers searched the refugees as they went.
- **The prize.** The fortress claim is the IM's existing prize.

## B-4 · Position 4 · The Table Laid

*Unique question 2 · Tuesday 13 April · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** In a hall of the palace, a long table is laid for a meal that no one came to eat: roast meats gone cold, bread, fruit, wine standing in gold and silver cups. A handful of servants stand against the wall, waiting to find out what kind of men you are.

**A.** Call your comrades in and feast.
> It is the best meal any of you has eaten since Venice. The story of the emperor's table goes round the camp for weeks.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas 0`

**B.** Sit the servants down and feed them first, then take them under your protection.
> They hesitate, then eat. When Henry's men come through, the servants are yours, and they show you which rooms are worth guarding.
`marks 50 · fama +1 · nefas -2`

**C.** Send for the hungry from the streets outside, and feed them.
> The hall fills with Greeks who have not eaten properly in weeks. It costs you nothing but the meal you might have had.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -3`

**D.** Sweep the gold and silver plate into a sack.
> The cups, the dishes and the salt cellars all go into the sack. The servants watch the meat go cold.
`marks 300 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Source note.** *Status: invented. You asked to keep this scene.*

- **What is real.** The surrender terms protected those inside the palace (Villehardouin §250), so a household would have been there to be found. The meal and the servants are invented.
- **Clari's feast.** Clari's only palace feast is Baldwin's coronation banquet on 16 May (ch. 97).

## B-5 · Position 5 · A Family Under Henry's Word

*Unique question 2 · 13–17 April · Tier: middling.*

**Prompt.** A Greek noble family who stayed in the palace under Henry's guarantee now want to leave the city, as the leaders allow. Soldiers in the streets are stopping anyone who looks rich. They ask you to walk them to the gate and offer you what they carry.

**A.** Escort them, and refuse payment.
> You walk them through three streets of soldiers to the gate. The father tries again to pay you there, and you send them on.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -2`

**B.** Escort them for the payment.

> They hand it over without a word. You don't learn whether they reached the gate.
`marks 250 · fama -2 · nefas +2`

**D.** Hold them, and send to their relatives for a ransom.
> The ransom comes in two days. You let them go to the gate with nothing.
`marks 300 · fama -1 · nefas +2`

- **Leaving.** Choniates (Mag. 322): the chiefs allowed those who wished to leave.
- **The streets.** Mag. 323–24: soldiers searched refugees for gold hidden in their clothing.

## B-6 · Position 6 · The Imperial Stables

*Unique question 2 · Tuesday 13 April · Tier: lowest · Repeatable.*

**Prompt.** The palace stables are full of horses and mules the Greeks could not take with them: tall chargers, ambling palfreys, strings of pack mules. Nobody seems sure whether horses count as booty that must be handed in.

**A.** Take a good horse for yourself.
> A palfrey fit for a bishop. If anyone asks, it followed you home.
`marks 100 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**B.** Take a string of mules to sell.
> Mules are always wanted, and these are good ones.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**C.** Lead them all to the collection point for the common stock.
> The clerks write them down. Someone grumbles that horses are not cloth or silver.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas 0`

**D.** Ride out on one in a courtier's robe, with a lady's veil tied over its head.
> Your comrades laugh. The Greeks you pass don't.
`marks 50 · fama -1 · nefas +1`

**Source note.** *Status: attested.*

- **Horses as booty.** Villehardouin ("The crusaders take a part of the city"): horses, palfreys and mules were captured on the 12th. §254: without theft and the Venetian share, the booty would have reached 400,000 marks of silver and at least 10,000 horses.
- **The ambiguity.** The oath (Clari ch. 68) named gold, silver and new cloth worth five shillings or more, not horses.
- **Option D.** Choniates (Mag. 326–27): Latins put fine linen veils and women's headdresses on their horses to mock the Greeks.

## B-Q3 · The Road Out

*Shared question 3 · Saturday 17 April · Every choice ends the same way: the refugees leave the city.*

**Prompt.** A storm is blowing, and a long column of Greeks is leaving by the gate: families carrying children too small to walk, a woman near her time, young women with mud smeared over their faces so no one will look twice. At the front, an official named Niketas Choniates is pleading with soldiers who are searching the refugees for gold sewn into their rags. Near the back, a gaunt old man in a single coat rides a donkey, and the people around him keep their eyes down.

**A · Piety.** Help Choniates get his people through the gate unharmed.
> Near the church of St Mokios a soldier snatches a judge's young daughter from the column, and her old father falls in the mud crying her name. Choniates begs you and the men with you to remember your oath. You go after the man together, and when you threaten to hang him he gives her up. The column passes out of the gate.
`marks 0 · fama +2 · nefas -1`

**B · Sacrilege.** Look harder at the old man on the donkey, then seize him.
> The people around him cry out as you drag the donkey's bridle round. He is the Patriarch of Constantinople, leaving his city with no staff and no shoes. He does not resist. Whatever the leaders decide about the Greek church, they will now decide it with him in their hands.
`marks 0 · fama +1 · nefas +2 · bonus: The Patriarch Taken (shared by every player who chooses B; see rule below) · tags: named-person`

**C · Violence.** Search the refugees yourself, and roughly, for what they are hiding.
> Gold in a hem, a ring in a mouth, a silver cross under a tunic. You find what others missed by not being gentle about it.
`marks 100 · fama -2 · nefas +1`

**D · Plunder.** Ignore them. Their houses inside the walls are empty now.
> You let the column go and work your way back along the streets they came from. Half the doors are open.
`marks 150 · fama 0 · nefas 0`

**Rule: The Patriarch Taken (proposed).** If any player chooses B, the patriarch has not escaped. He stays in the city under Latin guard.

- **Bonus.** Every player who chose B gains +1 fama, already in the line above, and is named in the narration as having taken part.
- **In Phase Four.** Whichever committee decides the church settlement may summon the patriarch. It may:
  1. Press him to submit to Rome. This meets the endgame line "Greek Patriarch forced to submit to Rome" without needing a new candidate.
  2. Keep him as Greek patriarch beside a Latin one, which is the dual patriarchate.
  3. Depose him and appoint a Latin.
- **Otherwise.** If no one chooses B, he leaves as he did historically, and the Greek church has a head outside Latin control.
- **Your call.** I've given the capture no individual votes, so that it can't multiply with class size. Tell me if you want that changed.

**Source note.** *Status: attested; moved from the Golden Gate to this gate for the game.*

- **Choniates' departure.** Mag. 323–24: Choniates left the city on Saturday 17 April, five days after its fall, in a winter storm, with his wife near childbirth. His party carried the small children and put the women in the middle. He told the young women to rub mud on their faces. Soldiers searched the passing captives for fine garments hidden under torn tunics and gold in their clothes, and some eyed the women.
- **The judge's daughter.** Mag. 324: at the church of St Mokios a soldier carried off a judge's daughter. Choniates followed him, appealed to passing troops by their own oath against violence to women, and when they threatened to hang the man, the girl was released.
- **The patriarch.** Mag. 325–26: the ecumenical patriarch went ahead of them, on a donkey, with neither staff nor sandals, in a single coat.
- **The gate.** Choniates went out by the **Golden Gate**, at the south end of the land walls, not by Blachernae. The gate is moved here to fit the game.
- **The patriarch's name and fate.** **M:** John X Kamateros, who died in Thrace without going to Nicaea. Both points are memory and need checking.
- **The Bulgarian thread.** Choniates' party ended up in Thrace (Selymbria, Mag. 326). Within a year the Greeks of Thrace allied with Ioannitsa (Kalojan), king of the Bulgarians and Vlachs, and together they defeated and captured the emperor Baldwin at Adrianople in April 1205 (Andrea's note to the *Deeds of the Bishops of Halberstadt*, in your sources file). How the refugees were treated is part of why the IM's endgame has Kalojan marching "backed by Greek rebels." Use this in the debrief, beside the Sack Aftermath's Bulgarian offer.
- **Care.** The abduction appears in the outcome of A only as a rescue. It is never a player action.
